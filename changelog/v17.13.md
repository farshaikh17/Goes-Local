# v17.13-shared — sub-categories: Google's own categories are joined into groups

Eric (20 Nov): "yes please" to the Miami mock-up (docs/mockups/all-subcategories.html; stores stay one group).

## Why
Each business's sub-category was its raw Google category: 790 in Miami, 271 with a single business, and look-alikes
("HVAC contractor" 259 / "Air Conditioning Contractor" 125 / "Air Conditioning Repair Service" 99). The category list also
had businesses under the wrong main ("Office space rental agency" under Beauty — "spa" inside "space"; "Carpet Installer"
under Pet Services — "pet" inside "carpet").

## Change (worker)
- New table `sub_group(sub_key, sub_slug, grp, main)`: one row per Google category → the site's group and main category.
  Empty table = nothing changes (Orlando and the states until their mapping is loaded).
- `norm()` applies it after the old rules: `cat = main`, `sub = grp`, so every sync keeps the grouped names.
- An old sub-category address (`/{cat}/{old-sub}`) that no longer exists 301s to its group's page (`sub_group.sub_slug`).
- Businesses that change main category get a new address under their new category; the old address is kept in
  `slug_redirects` (existing mechanism) and 301s.

## Loading the mapping (per city)
- Mapping file: docs/mockups/subcategory-mapping-miami.csv (Miami, 790 Google categories → 153 groups).
- Before loading: `sub_backup` (D1) keeps each listing's old cs/cat/sub/slug so it can be put back.
- Rollback: copy sub_backup back into businesses, delete the sub_group rows, bump the cache.
